Result in brief

  • The band-pass filter 25–150 Hz gives the smallest heart-rate error in this synthetic example [1].
  • At full volume the device overloads the output for the unfiltered signal; a fixed gain per filter avoids that.
  • Listen below: switch filters while the sample plays to hear the difference.

Why this matters

A digital stethoscope picks up heart sounds, but also breathing, skin rubbing and mains hum. A filter decides what the listener hears and what the heart-rate estimator sees [1, 2].

In plain words

band-pass filter

A filter that lets through only a range of pitches, here roughly the low hum of the heart (25 to 150 vibrations per second), and removes deeper rumble and higher hiss.

In plain words

clipping

When a signal is louder than the output can represent, its peaks are cut off flat. This sounds harsh and distorts the heart sounds.

Results

Finding

The band-pass filter reduced the mean heart-rate error from 9.8 to 1.9 BPM in this example [2].

Limitation

These numbers come from synthetic signals. They only demonstrate the page layout.

Project decision

The volume path stays unchanged; a fixed gain per filter is applied before it.

Open point
