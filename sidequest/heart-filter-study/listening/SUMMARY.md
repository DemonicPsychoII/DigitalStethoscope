# Listening-filter metrics: summary

Generated by `src/listening_metrics.py`. Grid: 236 candidates × 5 recordings, whole
recording after the first 2 s, volume 100 %, device chain from `hfs` (64-frame blocks, float32
biquads, firmware-exact volume stage and 24-bit saturation). Full tables: `metrics.csv` (per recording),
`metrics_summary.csv` (mean and worst case over recordings).

**The proxies do not replace listening.** They rank candidates by weighted energy; they do not know what a murmur
sounds like, how distortion or ringing is perceived, or how loud the earpieces really are. Use them to choose what to
listen to, then judge by ear with `samples/`.

## How the numbers are defined

- **Safe gain**: the largest single fixed gain (dB, 0.01 dB margin) after the filter for which *none* of the five
  recordings produces a sample at the 24-bit limits at the output (`metrics.csv` also has the per-recording value and
  the gain at which ≤ 0.01 % of samples saturate). It is limited by the recording named in `safe_gain_limiting_recording`.
- **Loudness proxies** (all relative to digital full scale, mean over the recordings, at the safe gain):
  A-weighted level per IEC 61672-1:2013 (A-weighting); K-weighted loudness per ITU-R BS.1770-4 (2015), gated
  integrated and ungated, in LUFS; and a level weighted with the inverse ISO 226:2003 equal-loudness contour at
  60 phon (relative to 1 kHz, extrapolated below 20 Hz at the 20–25 Hz slope; computed from the
  Welch power spectrum). The ISO 226 proxy is the closest of the three to hearing at a moderate listening level, but
  60 phon is an assumption, not a measured earpiece level. None of these is a loudness model
  (no ISO 532 / Zwicker or Moore-Glasberg model was used); they are proxies.
- BS.1770 gating: the recordings are short beats in relative quiet, and a 400 ms gating block nearly always contains
  part of a beat at these heart rates, so the relative gate (−10 LU) removes few blocks. Gated and ungated values are
  both in the CSV; over all candidates and recordings they differ by 0.17 LU (median) and at most
  1.94 LU. The ranking uses the gated value.
- **Other sound**: `between_to_heart_a_weighted_db` = A-weighted power in between-beat frames over that in heart-sound
  frames (lower = cleaner). Segments: reference beat times (reference/<name>.json) for all recordings.
  `energy_share_above_1khz_percent` and `energy_share_below_20hz_percent` are unweighted shares of output energy.
- **Transients**: group delay from the float32 coefficients; ringing = time until the envelope of the impulse response
  stays below −40 dB of its peak, for a click band-limited by a 4th-order 2 kHz Butterworth low-pass (the same click for
  every candidate, so Raw shows the click's own ~2 ms); step undershoot = largest negative step response relative to
  its peak (a high-pass always undershoots; larger means a deeper rebound after each beat).

## Findings from the numbers

- **Low high-pass edges buy no headroom.** 107 of 109 candidates with a high-pass at or below 40 Hz have a safe gain ≤ 0 dB: at gain 1.0 they already saturate on at least one recording (Raw: -0.01 dB, only because its input touches full scale). Removing the infrasound re-centres the hard-clipped, flat-topped overload beats and the movement artefact so that their audible part overshoots full scale. Limiting recording over all filtered candidates: elevated-2 165×, rest-1 70×.
- **Headroom grows only from about 50–60 Hz upwards** (Butterworth 2+2 with 800 Hz low-pass: 40 Hz -0.2 dB, 100 Hz +6.0 dB, 150 Hz +8.4 dB). The one fixed gain is set by the overload cases; on the clean rest-2 alone the design could take +9.6 dB.
- **Loudness at the safe gain exceeds Raw by the ISO 226 and A-weighted proxies only for high-pass edges of 100 Hz and above** (Butterworth 2+2, 800 Hz). The loudest candidate overall by the ISO 226 proxy is Butterworth 4+0 from 150 Hz at +4.3 dB over Raw. The design 40–800 Hz is at -1.5 dB (ISO 226) / -1.7 dB (A) relative to Raw.
- **The K-weighted loudness disagrees:** 0 filtered candidates are louder than Raw in LUFS. K-weighting is nearly flat down to about 40 Hz, so it counts the 20–100 Hz heart-sound energy that a high-pass removes, whereas at moderate listening levels the ear is far less sensitive there (ISO 226). Which proxy is closer depends on the real earpiece level, which is not known; listening decides.
- **The low-pass edge matters little for level:** from 200 Hz to no low-pass the ISO 226 proxy changes by +1.2 dB (high-pass 40 Hz). No candidate output carries more than 2.37 % of its energy above 1 kHz (mean over recordings), so the low-pass mainly affects audible hiss and friction noise, which energy numbers cannot rank.
- **Cleanliness:** the between-beat/heart ratio (A-weighted) is -14.9 dB for Raw and -14.3 dB for the design; higher high-pass edges make it worse (150 Hz, 2+2: -10.7 dB) because they remove the heart-sound fundamental but not the broadband noise between beats; a narrower low-pass makes it slightly better (40–200 Hz: -16.4 dB).
- **Transients:** 4th-order Chebyshev I and elliptic sections at 40–60 Hz have the longest ringing and group-delay spread (up to 33 ms across 40–400 Hz); Bessel and 2nd-order Butterworth the shortest. Whether that smears S1/S2 audibly is for listening.

Reasons:

- **Raw (no filter)** (`raw`): Reference: what the microphone delivers; the device's Raw position.
- **Butterworth 2+2 40-800 Hz** (`butterworth_hp2_40_lp2_800`): Current design candidate for Murmur; safe gain is near 0 dB because elevated-2 and the rest-1 movement artefact still reach full scale after a 40 Hz high-pass.
- **Butterworth 2+2 100-800 Hz** (`butterworth_hp2_100_lp2_800`): Same structure and cost as the design with the high-pass moved to 100 Hz: about 6 dB more fixed gain; tests whether the thinner, louder sound is preferred.
- **Butterworth 4+4 100-800 Hz** (`butterworth_hp4_100_lp4_800`): Steeper version of the 100 Hz band: more headroom and the higher loudness proxy at twice the cost; tests whether order matters audibly.
- **Butterworth 4+4 150-800 Hz** (`butterworth_hp4_150_lp4_800`): Extreme of the trade-off: the most fixed gain among the band-limited candidates, with most of the heart-sound fundamental removed; shows where it gets too thin.
- **elliptic 0.5/40 dB 4+4 60-800 Hz** (`elliptic_hp4_60_lp4_800`): Steep high-pass at a lower edge: keeps more body than 100 Hz while still gaining about 4 dB; long ringing and a large group-delay spread test whether S1/S2 sound smeared.
- **Butterworth 2+0 from 80 Hz** (`butterworth_hp2_80_lp0_none`): High-pass only, cheapest filter (5 multiplies): tests whether a low-pass is needed at all, since the recordings carry very little energy above 1 kHz.
- **Butterworth 2+2 25-150 Hz** (`butterworth_hp2_25_lp2_150`): The BPM switch position as it will sound; narrow band, cleanest for the beat but no murmur range.

The shortlist file `shortlist.json` drives `src/listening_samples.py`; edit it and rerun to change the listening set.
